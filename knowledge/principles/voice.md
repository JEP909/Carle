# Voice & Copy

The copy is part of the design. The references win on a specific register —
confident, concrete, benefit-led infrastructure language — never filler or
vibe-coder hype. Write the words as carefully as the layout.

## The register (grounded in Stripe / Linear / Chatbase / Chexy)
- **Confident + concrete.** Pair an ambitious claim with a real capability.
  Stripe: "Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue." Linear: "Purpose-built
  for modern teams." Chatbase: "The complete platform for AI support agents."
- **Benefit first, feature second.** Lead with the outcome for the user, then the
  mechanism. "Threats blocked before they land" > "Real-time ML inference."
- **Plain and active.** Short, declarative, present tense. No jargon soup, no
  exclamation marks, no emoji.
- **Trust cues where they fit.** "Enterprise-grade", "SOC 2", "99.99% uptime",
  real numbers — the references earn credibility with specifics, not adjectives.

## Mechanics
- **Headline:** one tight idea, ~3–7 words, sentence case. Not a slogan, not a
  pun. It should state the value, not decorate it.
- **Support line:** one or two muted lines that add a concrete detail (a number,
  a scope, a capability) — never restate the headline.
- **Labels/eyebrow:** a short uppercase category ("SECURITY", "PAYMENTS"), not a
  sentence.
- **CTA:** a clear action verb, often with a `→`. Grounded set: "Get started",
  "Book a demo", "Open app", "Start now", "See it in action". Pair a solid
  primary with a quiet ghost/text secondary.
- **Numbers are showpieces:** write real, specific figures ("4,847 threats",
  "11ms", "+643%") — round, fake-looking placeholders read as a mockup.

## Avoid (these read as AI slop)
"Unlock", "supercharge", "seamlessly", "elevate", "in today's fast-paced world",
"the power of", "revolutionary", "game-changing", "effortlessly", em-dash-bolded
hype phrases. Say the real thing instead.
